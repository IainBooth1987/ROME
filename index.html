Breakfast — keep it brief, Vatican by 9am
Prati · 5 options below depending on your mood
Espresso + cornetto ~2-3 standing at barVatican 10 min walk from hotel
›
This is a Vatican day so you cannot linger. The classic Roman breakfast is espresso and a cornetto (the Italian croissant — softer and sweeter than French, often filled with custard cream, jam or chocolate) standing at the bar. The whole thing takes 10 minutes and costs under €3. If you want something slightly more substantial, a maritozzo — Rome's iconic soft bun filled with whipped cream — is the local alternative. Pick your spot from the options below and get moving.
Wednesday breakfast — pick your spot 8:00-8:30am
Sciascia Caffe 1919
Best in Prati
Via Fabio Massimo, 80a · 5 min walk · Open 7am-8:30pm · £ budget ~€3-6pp
Over a century old and still the finest cafe in Prati. The pistachio cornetto is exceptional — light, fragrant, not too sweet. Their Fior di Neve (a pastry filled with ricotta cream) is unique to this cafe. In August, ask for a brioche con gelato — a soft brioche bun filled with gelato, deeply Roman and perfect in the heat. Stand at the bar for the authentic experience. A slight 5-minute detour but well worth it for the quality. Tel: 06 3210432.
Cremilla Gelateria e Pasticceria

Exceptional pastries and gelato, rated highly by locals. Perfect cappuccino and cornetto before the Vatican. Also useful on arrival night Tuesday if you are hungry after the transfer.
Commari

Friendly staff who speak English, excellent coffee, fresh Roman pastries that are genuinely good rather than tourist-grade rubbish. You can grab a cornetto and eat it standing on the way to the queue. Outdoor seating if you arrive 10 minutes early and want to sit briefly in the morning calm before St Peter's Square fills up.

Slightly slower than a quick espresso stop but more satisfying if you have had a good night's sleep and want to ease into the day. Near Castel Sant Angelo so easy to pass on the way to the Vatican.

Recommended

Trevi Fountain
Piazza di Trevi · ~15 min walk from Navona · best after 9pm
Evening magicThrow your coin together

From Piazza Navona: a straightforward 15-minute walk east through the historic centre — Via della Scrofa, then Via di Sant'Agostino, following signs for Fontana di Trevi. It is an easy and atmospheric walk through narrow streets. 9pm is significantly better than daytime — dramatically lit, crowds much thinner, genuinely romantic. Evening tip — the Colosseum is spectacular at night
Even if you don't go back inside, it is worth walking past the Colosseum after dark. The exterior is dramatically floodlit and the surrounding Via dei Fori Imperiali is one of Rome's most beautiful evening streets — the Imperial Fora lit on both sides, completely traffic-free in the evening. If you are heading to The Court for drinks, you get this view from the terrace. If not, even a 10-minute detour on the way to or from dinner is worthwhile. The stone turns amber under the lights in a way that makes 2,000 years of history feel completely real.

Cash tips: Carry 30-50 cash for street food, markets and small bars. Most restaurants and attractions take card. ATMs on Via Cola di Rienzo near your hotel. Avoid airport exchange — use Starling, Wise or Revolut for fee-free withdrawals.
Tipping in Rome
Romans do not tip the way Brits expect. A coperto (cover charge, 1.50-4pp) is automatically added to most sit-down meals — this is not a tip. Leaving a small amount (1-2pp) rounding up for good service is appreciated but entirely optional. Never feel obligated to tip 10-15% as you would at home. For coffee or street food: no tip expected at all.

Water — the nasoni secret

  • Rome has over 2,500 free drinking fountains called nasoni (little noses) — small iron spouts on virtually every street corner
  • The water is cold, clean and perfectly safe. Always carry a refillable bottle.
  • Buying bottled water repeatedly is expensive and unnecessary
  • In 32 degree heat: drink far more water than you think you need
  • Cold granita di caffe (coffee ice slush) from a bar is a brilliant mid-afternoon reviver — very Roman, very cheap

Ferragosto — what it means for your trip

Ferragosto (15 August) is Italy's biggest national holiday. You arrive 4 days after it but its effects linger well into the following week.
  • The good news: Romans leave the city in huge numbers for August — your trip (19-22 Aug) falls in the quieter post-Ferragosto window
  • The catch: Many neighbourhood trattorias close for 1-2 weeks around Ferragosto
  • Tourist-area restaurants (Prati, Navona area, Trastevere) largely stay open — your planned restaurants should be fine
  • Major attractions (Colosseum, Pantheon, Castel Sant Angelo) remain open
  • Public transport runs on a reduced summer timetable — allow extra time

What to wear

  • Fabric: Linen and cotton only — anything synthetic will be miserable. Light colours reflect heat.
  • Footwear: Comfortable walking shoes with support. Rome is cobblestones everywhere — flip flops will destroy your feet.
  • Churches including St Peter's Basilica and Colosseum: shoulders and knees must be covered. A lightweight scarf or sarong solves this instantly.
  • Evening: Rome dresses up for dinner. Gigi Rigolatto, Zuma and Les Etoiles expect smart-casual at minimum.
  • A small cross-body bag beats a rucksack in the heat and is pickpocket-resistant.

Emergency and pharmacy

  • Emergency number: 112 (all emergencies, English spoken)
  • Medical emergency: 118 (ambulance)
  • Nearest 24hr pharmacy: Farmacia Cola di Rienzo, Via Cola di Rienzo 213 — 5 min walk from hotel
  • Nearest A&E: Ospedale Santo Spirito, Lungotevere in Sassia 1 — 15 min walk
  • GHIC card: Bring it — entitles you to state healthcare at reduced cost
  • Heat exhaustion: Faint, dizzy, stops sweating — shade immediately, cool water on neck and wrists, seek help if no improvement in 15 mins

Getting around

  • Buy a 48hr or 72hr transit pass at Termini (~7/12) — covers metro, bus, trams
  • Line A: Lepanto (hotel) to Ottaviano (Vatican) to Spagna (Spanish Steps) to Termini
  • Line B: Termini to Colosseo (Colosseum, ~5 min)
  • Airport: Leonardo Express, Fiumicino to Termini, every 30 min, ~32 min, 14pp
  • Castel Sant Angelo: 10 min walk from hotel along the Tiber
  • Trastevere: Bus 23 from near hotel, or 25 min walk over Ponte Sisto
  • August public transport runs on a reduced Sunday-style timetable

Money and scams

  • Most places accept card but carry 30-50 cash for street food, markets and small bars
  • Coperto (cover charge, 1.50-4pp) is standard at sit-down restaurants — it is not a scam, it is normal
  • Tipping: Not expected the way it is in the UK. Rounding up 1-2 for good service is plenty.
  • Espresso at the bar: ~1.20. Sitting at a table can be 3-4x more — normal, not a tourist trap
  • Pickpocketing is common on public transport — zip bag, kept in front, on the metro
  • "Acqua del rubinetto" (tap water) is free to ask for in restaurants — do not feel pressured to buy bottled

Anniversary and birthday tips

Tell every restaurant it is a special occasion when booking. Romans love anniversaries and birthdays — expect something extra.
  • Trevi Fountain at 9pm — throw a coin together over your left shoulder with your right hand
  • Gigi Rigolatto rooftop — Pantheon dome lit up at night directly in front of you
  • Terrazza Les Etoiles aperitivo — St Peter's at sunset, two minutes from your hotel
  • Ponte Sant Angelo at dusk — Bernini angels leading to the lit Castel Sant Angelo, one of Rome's most romantic walks
  • Trastevere evening — medieval streets, ivy-covered walls, candlelit tables on cobblestones
  • A bottle of Prosecco from a supermarket (~8) for a private toast on your first night
