Knights of Malta Keyhole — Rome's best hidden secret
Piazza dei Cavalieri di Malta · Aventine Hill · Free
FreeBest photo of the tripShort queue · 5-10 min
›
Take a taxi from the Forum/Palatine Hill exit — about 5-7 min and ~€6. Do not walk in August heat: it is uphill all the way from Circus Maximus (~20 min on foot). The taxi drops you at Piazza dei Cavalieri di Malta. Look for the large wooden door of the Priory of the Knights of Malta on Piazza dei Cavalieri di Malta. There is usually a short informal queue of people waiting for a turn — join it.

When you reach the door, bend down and look through the keyhole. Through the keyhole you see a perfectly framed tunnel of hedges leading to St Peter's dome, precisely aligned in the centre. The dome appears close enough to touch despite being over 3 kilometres away. Three garden archways frame the view. It genuinely takes your breath away — the dome fills the entire keyhole circle perfectly, as if placed there by a set designer.

The whole thing takes 5-10 minutes including the queue. Free, no booking, no ticket.

5 minutes from the keyhole: Giardino degli Aranci — walk 5 min south along the Aventine to the Garden of Oranges (Parco Savello). A small garden of fragrant orange trees on the edge of the Aventine with one of the best views of Rome from its terrace — St Peter's dome directly in your eyeline, the Tiber below, and the dome of Sant'Ivo alla Sapienza to the right. Free, always open, rarely crowded. The combination of keyhole + Giardino degli Aranci + the walk downhill through the old Aventine streets to Testaccio is one of the finest 90-minute sequences in Rome that almost no visitor discovers.

After the keyhole and garden — walk downhill to Mercato Testaccio for lunch: From the Aventine it is a 10-minute walk downhill to Testaccio market — the best local food market in Rome. Far better than Campo de'Fiori (which is touristy) and more authentic than Mercato Trionfale. Then Metro from Piramide station back to Prati for the siesta. The whole morning flows naturally: Forum → Aventine keyhole → Testaccio lunch → Metro home.

Photo tip: Morning light is best — your ~12:30pm slot is good timing. Technique: get your phone lens right up to the keyhole (not your eye), set focus to infinity or tap the dome on screen to focus, and stabilise against the door frame. Standard camera mode not portrait. The three countries you see: Italy (where you stand), the sovereign territory of the Order of Malta (through the door), and Vatican City (the dome). No filter needed.

The heat — surviving August in Rome

August in Rome means 34-38°C every day with high humidity. Your body needs 24 hours to adapt. Your itinerary already has the structure right — early starts, long siesta, evenings out. Here is everything else that actually helps.
The fundamentals
  • The siesta is non-negotiable. 12:30-3:30pm indoors with AC. Do not try to push through the midday heat. The Romans figured this out centuries ago and your itinerary is built around it.
  • Go early or go late. Colosseum at 9:45am is correct. Vatican at 5:30pm is correct. Never do major outdoor sightseeing between 12pm and 4pm.
  • Drink constantly. You will dehydrate faster than you realise. Aim for 3-4 litres of water a day each. Carry a bottle everywhere.
  • Nasoni. Rome has 2,500 free drinking fountains throughout the city — small iron ones with a tube sticking up. The water is cold, clean and exceptional, fed from ancient aqueducts. Fill your bottle at every one you pass. Free and better than anything you can buy.
  • Wear linen or cotton only. Synthetics will make you miserable by 10am. Light colours, loose fit. A hat for the exposed sites — Colosseum, Forum, Palatine Hill.
Best places to cool down on your route
  • Churches. Thick ancient stone walls keep the interior significantly cooler. Walk into any church for 10 minutes to cool down. The Pantheon, St Peter's, Santa Maria in Trastevere — all free, all cool.
  • Villa Borghese lake. Shaded park, water, breeze. Rowboat on the lake Saturday morning is one of the coolest and most pleasant experiences of the trip.
  • Gianicolo Hill. Elevated, breezy and shaded. Significantly cooler than the streets below. This is where Romans actually go to escape the summer heat.
  • Giardino degli Aranci. Small shaded garden on the Aventine with fragrant orange trees and a breeze. On your Thursday morning route after the Knights of Malta keyhole.
  • Trastevere narrow streets. Medieval buildings on both sides create shade. Noticeably cooler than open piazzas.
  • The Metro. Air conditioned. Use it rather than walking long distances between 11am and 4pm.
Food and drink that genuinely helps
  • Granita al caffè con panna at Tazza d'Oro (near Pantheon) — cold coffee slush topped with whipped cream. Have one every afternoon. This is what it was invented for.
  • Acqua frizzante with every meal. Say "acqua frizzante per favore" — restaurants bring still unless you ask.
  • Gelato before the siesta — Gelateria dei Gracchi is 5 min from your hotel.
  • Aperol Spritz at aperitivo — low alcohol, ice-cold. The perfect 6pm drink in the heat.
  • Avoid heavy lunches. A trapizzino, pizza al taglio or panino works better than a three-course lunch in 35-degree heat. Save the big meals for the evening when it cools down.
Evening is the reward
  • Temperature drops to 22-24°C after sunset and the city comes completely alive. Your evening plans — Navona at 7pm, Terrazza Borromini rooftop, Trevi at 10:30pm, Trastevere for Cecilia — will all feel glorious after the day.
  • The Terrazza Borromini 6th floor at 8pm will have a pleasant evening breeze — a light layer is useful.

Practical: freeze water bottles in the hotel fridge the night before. Small portable fans are available at the pharmacy on Via Cola di Rienzo. SPF50 on any exposed skin, reapplied after sweating.

Water — the nasoni secret

  • Rome has over 2,500 free drinking fountains called nasoni (little noses) — small iron spouts on virtually every street corner
  • The water is cold, clean and perfectly safe. Always carry a refillable bottle.
  • Buying bottled water repeatedly is expensive and unnecessary
  • In 32 degree heat: drink far more water than you think you need
  • Cold granita di caffe (coffee ice slush) from a bar is a brilliant mid-afternoon reviver — very Roman, very cheap

Arguably Rome's most famous espresso bar — they whip the first drops with sugar into a thick cream before adding the rest. Ask for it senza zucchero if you want it unsweetened. Also sells their own freshly roasted beans to take home. Just steps from the Pantheon.
  • Roscioli Caffe (Piazza Benedetto Cairoli, 16) — espresso made with arabica beans from Torrefazione Giamaica Caffe, Italy's premier artisanal roaster. Also does pour-over and siphon coffee. Their maritozzi are legendary. Near Campo de'Fiori — perfect Thursday afternoon stop.

Further afield — worth a detour

Outdoor tables face directly onto Piazza del Popolo with the Egyptian obelisk in front of you and the twin churches framing the square. Beautiful at any time but extraordinary at 7:30am before the crowds. Best combined with the Terrazza del Pincio walk for the panoramic view of Rome (Thursday morning Option C). Cover charge for outdoor tables.
  • Antico Caffe Greco (Via Condotti, 86 — near Spanish Steps) — the oldest cafe in Rome, founded 1760. Frequented by Keats, Byron, Goethe and Wagner. Beautiful historic interior. Best for the experience rather than the coffee quality specifically.
Faro — Rome's specialty coffee pioneer
  • Faro Luminari del Caffe (Viale Aventino, 91/93) — widely regarded as the bar that brought specialty coffee to Rome. Roasts their own beans in-house (as Aliena Coffee Roasters). Single-origin espresso, cardamom buns, kouign-amann. Not on your main route but worth a detour — possibly on Thursday morning if you pass the Aventine for the Knights of Malta keyhole. Coffee ordering tips: Order at the bar (al banco) for the authentic experience and lower prices. A sitting-down surcharge is standard. "Un caffe" = espresso. "Caffe macchiato" = espresso with a drop of milk. "Cappuccino" only before noon in Italy — ordering one after lunch will raise eyebrows. "Caffe americano" is available but frowned upon. "Caffe freddo" = cold espresso. The granita al caffe in summer is the insider move.

Street food & eating in the sun — the best spots on your route

Rome's street food scene is genuinely world-class. The rule is simple: queue = quality. Any place with a man outside waving you in or a picture menu is to be avoided.
Must-try Roman street food
  • Pizza al taglio (by the slice, pay by weight) — Bonci Pizzarium is the benchmark but any good pizzeria al taglio is worth stopping at. Look for one with a queue of locals at lunchtime.
  • Trapizzino (triangular pizza dough pocket with Roman fillings) — a modern Roman invention. Chicken cacciatore, meatballs in tomato, braised offal. Try at Trapizzino near Testaccio or Bere at Piazza Risorgimento (Prati).
  • Neve di Latte (Via Trionfale, 28 — Prati, 10 min from hotel) — arguably the finest gelato in Rome. Order fior di latte and hazelnut — the simple flavours reveal the quality. Almost entirely local crowd. Easy to combine with any Prati evening.
  • Dar Filettaro a Santa Barbara (Largo dei Librari, 88 — 2 min from Campo de'Fiori) — battered salt cod (baccalà) in paper cones, fried since 1938, serving virtually nothing else. Crispy outside, flaky inside. Eat immediately with lemon. ~€4 and completely unlike anything else in Rome.
  • Supplizio (Via dei Banchi Vecchi, 135 — near Campo de'Fiori) — gourmet supplì in truffle, squid ink, carbonara and classic ragù versions. The artisan take on Rome's favourite fried rice ball. ~€3-4 each.
  • Supplì (fried rice balls with tomato ragù and mozzarella) — pull them apart and the mozzarella stretches (hence "telefono"). Best eaten hot from a paper bag. Look for the queue outside any suppli bar.
  • Carciofi alla romana (Roman artichokes) or alla giudia (Jewish-style, deep fried) — Rome's signature vegetable. Best in spring but available in August. Campo de'Fiori area has the best versions on your Thursday afternoon wander.
  • Maritozzo (soft Roman bun filled with whipped cream) — the Roman equivalent of a cream bun. Served at almost every bar. Best from Roscioli Caffe or Forno Campo de'Fiori.
  • Granita al caffe con panna (coffee slush with whipped cream) — the ultimate August street drink. Get it from Tazza d'Oro near the Pantheon. Transformative in 34-degree heat.

Gelato — how to spot the good stuff

Most gelato near tourist sites is factory-made. Signs of genuine artisan gelato: natural colours (pistachio should be grey-green not neon green), covered containers, and a short ingredient list.

Aperitivo — Rome's evening ritual

Aperitivo is one of the best things about Italy. Between roughly 6pm and 9pm, bars offer a drink which comes with free food — ranging from a small bowl of olives up to an unlimited buffet. You are paying for the drink, the food is the bonus.

A spritz (Aperol or Campari, prosecco, soda) is the classic — refreshing, low alcohol, and deeply Roman. A negroni (gin, Campari, sweet vermouth) is the more sophisticated alternative.
  • Freni e Frizioni (Trastevere) — consistently rated Rome's best aperitivo. One drink (~€10) unlocks an unlimited vegetarian buffet. Arrive at 7pm. No reservations.
  • Salotto 42 (Piazza di Pietra, near Pantheon) — the most stylish option near your Thursday evening route. Facing the Temple of Hadrian columns. Cocktails ~€12-15.
  • Bar del Fico (Via della Pace, behind Navona) — most affordable near your Wednesday evening route. Spritz ~€7 with snacks. Fig tree courtyard.
  • I Pizzicaroli (Via della Fossa, 9 · 2 min from Pantheon) — sharing boards of bruschetta, cured meats and cheeses with a spritz. Spritz €6, boards €15-20. No booking, just turn up.
  • Terrazza Les Etoiles (Prati, 2 min from hotel) — rooftop option. Fixed €45pp includes cocktail and finger food. St Peter's dome in front of you.

Aperitivo runs 6-9pm. Arrive early for the best buffet selection. Romans eat dinner at 8-9pm, so aperitivo bridges the gap.

Ferragosto — what it means for your trip

Ferragosto (15 August) is Italy's biggest national holiday. You arrive 4 days after it but its effects linger well into the following week.
  • The good news: Romans leave the city in huge numbers for August — your trip (19-22 Aug) falls in the quieter post-Ferragosto window
  • The catch: Many neighbourhood trattorias close for 1-2 weeks around Ferragosto
  • Tourist-area restaurants (Prati, Navona area, Trastevere) largely stay open — your planned restaurants should be fine
  • Major attractions (Colosseum, Pantheon, Castel Sant Angelo) remain open
  • Public transport runs on a reduced summer timetable — allow extra time

What to wear

  • Fabric: Linen and cotton only — anything synthetic will be miserable. Light colours reflect heat.
  • Footwear: Comfortable walking shoes with support. Rome is cobblestones everywhere — flip flops will destroy your feet.
  • Churches including St Peter's Basilica and Colosseum: shoulders and knees must be covered. A lightweight scarf or sarong solves this instantly.
  • Evening: Rome dresses up for dinner. Gigi Rigolatto, Zuma and Les Etoiles expect smart-casual at minimum.
  • A small cross-body bag beats a rucksack in the heat and is pickpocket-resistant.

Uber as you know it does not exist in Rome. But taxis are easy and two local apps make them even easier. Download both before you travel.
Official taxis — white cars, metered
  • Rome taxis are white with a "TAXI" light on the roof. You cannot flag them down on the street. You must find a taxi stand (fermata taxi) or use an app. Stands are at every major sight, piazza and train station — easy to find in the daytime.
  • The meter starts when you get in. Drivers must legally accept card payment, though some claim their machine is broken — insist, or pay cash and get a receipt.
  • Always confirm the driver starts the meter. If they refuse or quote a price much higher than expected, get out.
  • Typical fares: within central Rome €8-15 · hotel to Navona area ~€12-15 · Vatican to Trastevere ~€10-14 · hotel to Colosseum ~€12-15 · Fiumicino Airport to city centre: fixed €55 flat rate (no meter for airport runs).
Best apps — download both before you go
  • FreeNow — the one to use. Jointly owned by BMW and Mercedes-Benz, works exactly like Uber at home. Open the app, enter your destination, see the price estimate upfront, confirm the ride, driver comes to you. Pay in-app. Official licensed taxis at metered prices. Works very well in Rome, especially useful after dinner when you don't want to find a taxi stand at 11pm in an unfamiliar area.
  • ItTaxi — the official Italian taxi association app. Works equally well. Use this as a backup if FreeNow is unavailable. Both are free to download.
  • Uber app — does work in Rome but only Uber Black (expensive limo service). However, the app now integrates with ItTaxi, so if you open Uber and choose the "Taxi" option rather than Uber Black, you will get a regular white cab at normal meter prices. This is the closest thing to a standard Uber ride available in Rome.
Scams to avoid
  • At Fiumicino arrivals: Men in suits will approach you in the arrivals hall offering "taxi" rides for €80-100+. Walk straight past all of them. Go outside to the official white taxi rank and pay the fixed €55 fare. The official rank is clearly signposted.
  • No meter: If a driver refuses to start the meter or quotes a suspiciously high price, do not get in or get out immediately.
  • Wrong route: Have your destination in Google Maps before getting in so you can see if the driver is taking an unnecessarily long route.
When to use taxis vs Metro
  • Metro — best for longer journeys along the A or B lines (hotel to Colosseum, hotel to Spagna). Fast, cheap (~€1.50), air-conditioned in summer. Runs until 11:30pm Sunday-Thursday, 1:30am Friday-Saturday.
  • Taxi/FreeNow — best for off-line journeys (Navona to Testaccio, Vatican to Trastevere), after dinner when you want door-to-door, or when carrying bags.
  • Walking — underrated. The historic centre is compact. Navona to Pantheon is 5 min. Pantheon to Trevi is 10 min. Colosseum to Monti is 5 min. Always check on Maps before paying for a taxi.

Surge pricing can apply on FreeNow and Uber Taxi during peak evening times. If the price looks high, wait 5 minutes and try again, or walk to the nearest taxi stand instead.

Getting around

  • Buy a 48hr or 72hr transit pass at Termini (~7/12) — covers metro, bus, trams
  • Line A: Lepanto (hotel) to Ottaviano (Vatican) to Spagna (Spanish Steps) to Termini
  • Line B: Termini to Colosseo (Colosseum, ~5 min)
  • Airport: Leonardo Express, Fiumicino to Termini, every 30 min, ~32 min, 14pp
  • Castel Sant Angelo: 10 min walk from hotel along the Tiber
  • Trastevere: Bus 23 from near hotel, or 25 min walk over Ponte Sisto
  • August public transport runs on a reduced Sunday-style timetable

Money and scams

  • Most places accept card but carry 30-50 cash for street food, markets and small bars
  • Coperto (cover charge, 1.50-4pp) is standard at sit-down restaurants — it is not a scam, it is normal
  • Tipping: Not expected the way it is in the UK. Rounding up 1-2 for good service is plenty.
  • Espresso at the bar: ~1.20. Sitting at a table can be 3-4x more — normal, not a tourist trap
  • Pickpocketing is common on public transport — zip bag, kept in front, on the metro
  • "Acqua del rubinetto" (tap water) is free to ask for in restaurants — do not feel pressured to buy bottled

Anniversary and birthday tips

Tell every restaurant it is a special occasion when booking. Romans love anniversaries and birthdays — expect something extra.
  • Trevi Fountain at 9pm — throw a coin together over your left shoulder with your right hand
  • Gigi Rigolatto rooftop — Pantheon dome lit up at night directly in front of you
  • Terrazza Les Etoiles aperitivo — St Peter's at sunset, two minutes from your hotel
  • Ponte Sant Angelo at dusk — Bernini angels leading to the lit Castel Sant Angelo, one of Rome's most romantic walks
  • Trastevere evening — medieval streets, ivy-covered walls, candlelit tables on cobblestones
  • A bottle of Prosecco from a supermarket (~8) for a private toast on your first night
